One thing that costs your customer, not you

Move a customer from deployer to provider
without telling them.

A company using AI under its own authority is a deployer. One that puts a system into service under its own name, or substantially modifies one, becomes a provider — carrying a far heavier set of legal duties.

Still a deployer

You install your agent, configured, under your name. Your customer operates it.

The heavy provider duties stay largely with you.

Duties stay with vendor

Now a provider

You fine-tune the model on their data, or ship it under their brand.

They have substantially modified a system — and the heavier duties are now theirs.

Heavier duties shift to client

That difference belongs in the SOW. Scoping conversation, not post-mortem.

The Invisible Legal Tripwire: Slap your bank's logo on it or fine-tune on customer emails → you inherit the vendor's full Provider liability. Press M to toggle.

Q&A Backup 01 · Gap 01 / RFC-001

AI Kill Switch: Hard Stop vs. "Dimmer Switch"

CROWDSTRIKE FALCON GUARDIAN · NOV 2026

4-Layer Enforcement Pattern

01 · AI Gateway Cut-Off — Block model/provider access via connected gateways (Composite Gate fail-closed).

02 · Root Process Kill — Terminate rogue running agent where it executes (cancel active context.Context trees).

03 · Relaunch Prevention — Block orchestrator auto-restart loops.

04 · Network Isolation — Revoke downstream credentials & log blast radius.

BANKING · TIERED CONTAINMENT

Stop Agents Without Freezing Payments

Tier 1 · Dimmer Switch — Degrade agent to read-only, strip high-risk tools (ASI02), or force HITL above a cap.

Tier 2 · Identity / NHI Revocation — Instantly revoke short-lived OAuth tokens & IAM keys (ASI03) to sever core rail access.

Tier 3 · Operator Hard Stop — Broadcast fail-closed scope stop, cancel in-flight workflows, append KILL_SWITCH_ON to SHA-256 chain.

RBI Draft MRM Guidance: requires client-side kill-switch capability even on third-party vendor models. See docs/open-problems/RFC-001-ai-kill-switch.md
